Influence ranking and anomaly detection in networks are usually handled by separate methods, and neither explains why a node scores as it does. This project asks whether the neighbour weights learned by a graph attention network (GAT) can serve both tasks, and tests the idea instead of assuming it. We implement a shared two-layer multi-head GAT encoder (with GATv2 and GCN controls) on attributed graphs, together with an attention-only anomaly score, a reconstruction score, a combined score, a received-attention influence score with and without degree adjustment, and a small influence head trained on independent-cascade simulations. Everything runs on a laptop CPU. We evaluate on the Weibo organic-anomaly benchmark (8,405 users, 868 labelled anomalous; we validated the data against a pinned upstream commit and found that the current upstream file blanks the validation and test labels, so we use the original 2022 file) and on Cora with seeded injected anomalies, using three and five seeds, against Isolation Forest, a DOMINANT-style GCN, degree, PageRank and betweenness, plus non-attentive controls. Influence is judged against held-out simulated spread, which is a proxy and not observed influence. The attention-only score reaches {{fact:cora_injected.anom.gat_anom.att_only.roc_auc.pm}} on Cora but is matched by a uniform-weight control ({{fact:cora_injected.anom.gat_anom.feat_dev_uniform.roc_auc.pm}}), and on Weibo it is weaker ({{fact:weibo.anom.gat_anom.att_only.roc_auc.pm}}). Received attention does not beat degree for influence (Spearman {{fact:weibo.inf.0.003.gat_joint.recv_att.spearman.pm}} against {{fact:weibo.inf.0.003.centrality.degree.spearman.pm}} on Weibo) and equals a uniform-attention control on Cora, while a plain GCN influence head performs best ({{fact:weibo.inf.0.003.gcn_joint.model_pred.spearman.pm}} on Weibo). Removing high-attention edges lowers anomaly scores more than removing random edges on both datasets, and lowers influence predictions more on Weibo, where attention is sharp, but not on Cora, where it is nearly uniform; attention therefore describes what the trained model uses without being a better predictor than simple controls. In these experiments joint training gave no reliable benefit. The conclusions are limited to these graphs, proxies and CPU budgets.
